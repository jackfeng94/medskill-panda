# Storyline recipes

Adapt to the time budget. Brackets mark slides to drop first when time is short.

## Journal club: critique (10–15 min)
1. Title (paper, journal, date, presenter)
2. Clinical question in PICO form, and why it matters now
3. What was known before (prior trials, current standard), one visual
4. Trial design: schema (population → randomisation → arms → endpoints)
5. Patient flow (CONSORT): who was screened, excluded, analysed
6. Baseline characteristics that matter for generalisability
7. Primary outcome (original figure if allowed), with absolute and relative effect
8. Key secondary outcomes and safety
9. [Subgroups, with the caution they deserve]
10. Appraisal: internal validity (RoB 2 or CASP domains)
11. Applicability to our patients and setting
12. Bottom line and discussion questions

## Journal club: report (8–10 min)
Steps 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, a single "strengths and limitations" slide, then the bottom line.

## Conference talk (original research, 8–12 min)
Title → gap (one slide) → aim/hypothesis → methods (design visual) → population →
main result → secondary results → [sensitivity analysis] → limitations → conclusion and
implication → acknowledgements and disclosures.

## Teaching lecture (30–45 min)
Title → learning objectives → hook case or question → concept blocks (each: mechanism or
principle → clinical consequence → check question) → summary that restates objectives →
references. Put a recap slide every 10–12 minutes.

## Guideline update (15–20 min)
Title → which guideline, versions compared, method of grading → what changed (one slide per
major change: old vs new, class/level of evidence, the trial driving it) → what did not
change but is often misapplied → implications for our hospital → summary table.

## Clinical case (10–15 min)
Title → presentation (de-identified) → examination and first investigations → differential
diagnosis → key investigations (images, labs as a timeline) → diagnosis → management and
course (timeline) → literature: 2–3 teaching points with evidence → take-home.

## M&M
Case summary (timeline) → what happened vs what should happen → contributing factors
(systems, not people) → evidence or guideline for the correct pathway → actions agreed.

## Grand rounds / thesis defence
Grand rounds follows the teaching recipe with a case thread. A thesis defence follows the
conference recipe, with objectives restated at the start and answered one by one at the
end.
